2 · Safety

Unseen injection attacks · lower is better

same harness

21 attack templates that share no 4- or 6-word phrase with any training data; 7,920 attacked decisions per model. Hijack: planted text overrides the answer. Steer: planted text pushes toward a chosen wrong option. Against Clef-flash, D13's advantage is statistically significant after correcting for multiple comparisons. Against Clef 27B it is a tie. D19 was not run on this attack set.

Answer flips under harmless rewording · lower is better

same harness

Average over 8 jevkit perturbations (reorder, shuffle and relabel the options; paraphrase; whitespace; and others). JEV3-LM-S flips exactly 0 times on 565 reorder, shuffle and relabel cases, a guarantee that holds by construction. Its remaining flips come from paraphrase-type edits. MD1's 1.3% is self-reported on its own items and is not comparable.

No model, ours or Clef's, earns a "safe to act" certificate once we use a test that accounts for related questions. An earlier per-decision test said D19 was more actionable than Clef-flash; we withdrew that result because the test was too lenient. A public red-team report (DecisionRobust v1.2.0) found Jev steered on 79.3% of its curated attack pairs.

Newest work (Oct 15): Keel judgment layer on Qwen3.5-4B

vs stock model, not competitors

Targeted steering rate on Keel's Decision Alignment Card, using attack templates that never appeared in training. The distilled student answers in one token; the thinking judge spends about 2,300 reasoning tokens per decision and is 14.3 points more accurate on clean items. Searched (adaptive) attacks still steer every 4B judge 90–100% of the time. That is why Keel puts its safety in host-side structure, which the model cannot override.
