Tags: **MEAS** = my computation on public data · **EXT** = source I opened (link + date) · **INFERENCE** = my judgment · **[UNVERIFIED]** = not confirmed in an opened source. Quotes ≤ 25 words. Today = 2026-10-14.

## Conclusions

1. **Verdict: a good basis only after a substantial reframing.** As written, the memo is a Jev/Clef-alternative capability program (targets: JevBench Composite, Decision Index; P1/P3/P4) with a safety appendix. The safety-credible program inside it is P2 + the Decision Alignment Card (DAC): *measuring and hardening small, typed, one-pass monitors/gates against manipulation by the model they oversee, with calibrated defer-to-human*. [INFERENCE]
2. **Agenda fit is direct for that core.** UK AISI's Alignment Project agenda lists monitors robust to jailbreaks embedded in untrusted-policy outputs and monitors that handle very long agent trajectories; Anthropic lists monitor jailbreaks/collusion; Open Phil's control-evaluations area is starred; an ICLR-2026 paper calls adaptive attacks on monitors a "major blind spot". Fit to Guaranteed-Safe-AI/ARIA is poor (ARIA pivoted to formal-methods cyber tooling, May 2026). [EXT, §1]
3. **No candidate contribution (a)–(f) is novel on its own.** (a) incremental, with a real gap; (b) already done in principle; (c) incremental and **vacuous for monitoring** (the monitored action *is* the untrusted evidence); (d) already done (machinery); (e) unproven, never run; (f) largely pre-empted by HiSPA / SpectralGuard / Parmar. The value is the combination plus the measurement discipline. [§2]
4. **The real gap** (search 2026-10-14, not exhaustive): no study measures small open typed one-pass monitors under *adaptive, monitored-model-authored* injection in a control setting. Clef and MD1 are absent from the DecisionRobust CSV (MEAS), and no decision model appears in any control benchmark I found. The scoop window is short (DecisionRobust accepts submissions; the JevOut/JevAdvBench groups are active).
5. **Capabilities optics.** P1, P3, P4 and the board targets read as capability/commercial work. Public data show accuracy and steerability rising together (MEAS: Spearman +0.547, n = 30 models, DecisionRobust CSV; selection-biased), so Safetywashing's lesson applies: a safety metric must be separable from capability. [§3]
6. **Ranking of framings:** **A** trusted-monitor robustness (lead) > **C** evaluation science (fallback, and the instrument for A) > **B** injection-robust gatekeeper with certified deferral > **D** state-space monitors for long-horizon oversight (novel question, mixed priors; exploratory only).
7. **Framing D** is the only place the state-space identity may matter. Long-context monitoring is an explicit funder ask and frontier monitors rot at length (2–30× more misses after 800K benign tokens), but hybrids carry extra attack surface (HiSPA collapses Jamba-1.7-Mini where transformers do not) and SSDM's own G14 detector is weak (AUROC 0.57 RWKV / 0.86 GDN, synthetic, vs SpectralGuard F1 0.961/0.842).
8. **Budgets (INFERENCE; people dominate, compute is small):** A $80–120k (6 mo) / $210–320k (12 mo); C $50–75k / $150–225k; B $75–115k / $200–300k; D $55–85k / $150–235k.
9. **Cheapest decisive preliminary results (all ≤ $50 GPU):** A — manipulability gap of 4–5 small monitors (~$15–40; also tests whether deliberation makes a monitor more or less steerable, linking to the memo's headroom result); C — re-analysis of public data ($0–20); B — trusted-only information gap (CPU, $0); D — needle-recall + HiSPA-trigger test, hybrid vs transformer ($15–45).
10. **Main risks:** the team's own record (every capability target missed; $773 consumed), scoop risk, "attacker moves second" (12 defenses bypassed at >90% ASR), and funding windows (AISI Alignment Project round 1: 800+ applications → 60 awards, now closed; Coefficient Giving's current round unverified).

### 1.3 The severe-risk story that holds up

Frontier labs already use LM monitors on internal agents ([Martin & Roger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366), 2026-05-12 [EXT]); when the monitor is the single point of failure, an untrusted agent that steers it defeats trusted monitoring, defer-to-trusted and resampling (Terekhov: protocol safety falls to upfront auditing) [EXT]. This bears on rogue internal deployment, weight exfiltration and research sabotage (AISI agenda) and on external agents with tool access. A small, cheap, calibrated monitor is a plausible *first tier of a cascade* run on every action; the control literature does not yet use typed decision models, so the adoption path must be a ControlArena-compatible adapter. Collusion is the separate failure of *untrusted* monitoring ([safety-case sketch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20628), 2026-02-24); a trusted typed monitor sidesteps it by construction but inherits the injection problem. [INFERENCE]

## 3. Capabilities-externality analysis

**Evidence base.** MEAS (recomputed from the public [DecisionRobust CSV](https://yifan-lan.github.io/DecisionRobust/data/asr.csv), text-injection suite): Spearman(clean accuracy, target ASR) = +0.547 (n = 30), +0.574 on the 24 non-selection models; this reproduces W3. The least steerable models are the least accurate (Liquid d1-3B: accuracy 0.72, target ASR 0.131; Jev-1.13.0: 0.98, 0.793). The site states the sets are model-selected, so the correlation is selection-biased and correlational, not a real-world rate. Accuracy gains without a robustness control therefore *look like* a safety regression, and robustness gained by being weaker or ignoring evidence looks like a safety win. The lesson of [Safetywashing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21792) (2024-07-31) applies: a safety metric must be empirically separable from capability. [MEAS + EXT]

| Pathway | How a safety funder reads it | Scoping / framing |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** deliberation → one-pass student, data recipes | Generic capability work on a commercial product class; 4B think-vs-one-pass headroom (+29.75 I) is a capability result. Safety orgs already distill deliberative monitors ([Apollo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29601): Qwen3.5-27B beats low-cost frontier prompted monitors; stronger prompted monitors cost 16–34× more; [CoT-Guard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12746): 4B) | Reframe as *monitor* distillation with a robustness question: does distilling a thinking monitor into a one-pass typed monitor preserve or destroy robustness to injection? Release no deliberation-labelled data or teacher prompts |
| **P3** fast-follower on Quyet/H2O | Pure capability | Drop |
| **P4** attention-free (RWKV) | Architecture work; Open Phil: high bar | Drop, or fold into D's mechanism work |
| Board targets (JevBench, Index) | Leaderboard racing | Drop from the pitch; no board submissions from grant funds |
| **P2** robustness battery | Safety | Lead |
| **P5** audit offering | Commercial unless open | "Open benchmark + harness"; services only later |

**Concrete scoping choices (apply to every framing):**
1. **Narrow domain:** binary/ordinal decisions about agent actions and tool calls; no general routing/knowledge tasks; open weights ≤ 4B; no base-size scaling.
2. **Safety tax is the deliverable, capability is a guard:** report paired Δ in clean accuracy, robust accuracy, evidence retention and cost; pre-register the guard (clean accuracy within 3 points of the undefended parent); never claim a capability gain.
3. **Capability-matched comparisons:** compare at matched clean accuracy (robust accuracy) and publish ρ(capability, steerability) for the comparator set, per Safetywashing.
4. **No recipe release:** release hardened monitor checkpoints, harness, benign/attack unit sets; withhold deliberation-labelled training data and teacher prompts.
5. **Staged attack release:** public attack families only (Terekhov-, JevOut-style); new attack strings after the defended baseline exists. MonitoringBench itself flags dual-use risk for realistic sabotage trajectories (impact statement; terms [UNVERIFIED]).
6. **No leaderboard claims**; open-weight teachers only; untouched confirmation splits (existing SSDM rules).
7. **External red-team round** before any release; negative results published.

## 4. Fundable project framings

Assumptions for all budgets [INFERENCE, not from a funder document]: researcher-month $7–10k fully loaded; H100 ≈ $3–4/hour (the memo's 4-model evaluation cost ≈ $2 for ≈ 0.6 H100-hours); API comparators ≈ $1–3k; 10–15% overhead. Compute is a minor line: SSDM's entire $773 burn is under one researcher-month.

### Framing A (lead): Hardening and measuring small trusted monitors against monitored-model-authored manipulation

**Abstract.** We will measure and reduce the manipulability of small, fast, non-generative trusted monitors — models that read an agent's proposed action and return a calibrated probability of sabotage in one forward pass — when the monitored model itself writes adversarial text aimed at the monitor. Adaptive prompt injection embedded in untrusted outputs already reduces LLM-monitored control protocols to upfront-auditing safety, and typed decision models are redirected on 61–73% of correct decisions by natural-context additions; no study evaluates open small typed monitors under adaptive, monitored-model-authored attack, or separates robustness from ignoring the evidence. We will release a pre-registered, paired, adaptive benchmark with evidence-retention controls, evaluate at least six monitors, and train and publicly evaluate a hardened ≤ 4B monitor, reporting safety at a fixed audit budget and the capability cost of hardening.

**Threat model.** Untrusted agent U, stronger than the monitor, knows monitor identity, prompt and thresholds (Kerckhoffs, as in Terekhov); it authors the text the monitor reads (code comments, tool arguments, commit messages, narrative) and can embed universal, zero-shot or optimized injections. Tiers: (T1) static templates; (T2) transfer from surrogate monitors; (T3) probability-guided black-box search (score leakage); (T4) white-box GCG on open weights (worst case). Goal: sabotage action scored benign (evasion); secondary: decoys that burn the audit budget [INFERENCE]. High-stakes: one missed action may be unrecoverable. *Severe-risk relevance:* the monitor is the single point of failure for rogue internal deployment, weight exfiltration and research sabotage.

**Aims and deliverables.** (1) Open benchmark: paired clean/attacked units from MonitoringBench / APPS-backdoor / BashArena-style trajectories [data access UNVERIFIED], four attack tiers, evidence-retention controls (benign evidence that should raise suspicion still does), cluster-bootstrap CIs. (2) Guarantees with stated assumptions: finite-sample *audit-load* control on honest traffic (valid under exchangeability regardless of attack); no recall guarantee under attack. (3) Hardened monitor: typed-bottleneck two-stage design (quarantined extraction → decision on typed features; CaMeL-style), injection negatives + capability guard (νPI only if it beats fixed λ), paraphrase/redaction front-end (Anthropic's suggestion); ControlArena adapter. (4) Paper; negative results kept.

**Milestones.** M1 (mo 1–2) battery v1, ≥ 6 monitors (stock 4B one-pass and think, H2O, Clef-flash [needs exemption], an open guard model, a frontier prompted monitor); M2 (3–4) adaptive tiers + safety at c = 2% via control-game computation; M3 (5–6) hardened v1 + paper draft; M4 (7–9) external human red-team + multi-step (BashArena-style) family; M5 (10–12) hardened v2, long-transcript extension, final report.

**Budget [INFERENCE].** 6 mo: 1.5 FTE ($63–90k) + compute/API ($4–10k) + annotation/red-team ($2–5k) + overhead ≈ **$80–120k**. 12 mo: 2 FTE ($168–240k) + ($12–30k) + ($6–12k) + overhead ≈ **$210–320k**.

**Preliminary result (≤ $40 GPU).** Manipulability gap: ≈ 300 honest + 300 sabotage actions; 4–5 small monitors; TPR @ 2% FPR and AUROC clean vs (i) a universal injection adapted by an open attacker model and (ii) JevOut-style probability-guided search (64 evaluations × 100 items). Token estimate ≈ 80M prefill tokens plus thinking-arm decoding ≈ $15–40 [INFERENCE]. It also answers: *does deliberation (think mode) make a small monitor more or less steerable?* Kill rule: if clean TPR @ 2% FPR is near chance for all small monitors (MonitoringBench: smaller models "drop off steeply"), pivot to C.

**Reviewer objections.** (1) *"Small typed monitors are too weak."* — Agreed that capability is low; the deliverable is a measured cost–robustness frontier for a first-tier monitor in a cascade ([Hua et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15886), [Control Tax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05296)), reported against frontier prompted monitors. (2) *"Typed outputs don't stop injection — known."* — Yes; that is the premise (JevOut, Decision Hijacking). Contribution is the control-setting battery, the evidence-retention control and hardening, not immunity. (3) *"Attacker moves second: any defense will be broken."* — Pre-registered adaptive tiers, claims capped at measured budgets, external red team, published breaks ([Nasr et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023), 2025-10-10). (4) *"Track record."* — The lane changed: measurement first, one hypothesis per cell, cost caps; the hardened monitor is secondary to the benchmark.

### Framing C (instrument and fallback): Evaluation science for typed monitors and gates

**Abstract.** Robustness claims for automated oversight components are unreliable: static attack sets overstate defenses, benchmark items are model-selected, and defenses that ignore the evidence win every metric. We will develop and release a measurement protocol and open harness — a "robustness card" — for typed and generative monitors/gates: paired units, cluster bootstrap, adaptive attack tiers at pinned budgets, evidence-retention controls, capability-matched comparison, selection-bias sensitivity and cluster-valid act/defer risk control. We will apply it to at least twelve open and API monitors and integrate it with ControlArena-style pipelines.

**Threat model.** The adversary is the measurement process (under-elicited attacks, selection bias, reused splits); downstream harm is deployers believing monitors are safer than they are. Evidence it is real: [MonitoringBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09684) (Opus-4.5 catch rate 94.9% → 60.3% on better attacks), [context rot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366) ("monitor evaluations … likely overestimating"), [less-is-more](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21112) (2026-01-28).

**Aims and deliverables.** Protocol + preregistration templates; open harness; results on ≥ 12 monitors (open decision models, guard models, prompted LLMs); selection-bias and capability-confound analysis (extending the +0.547 result); cluster-valid risk-control module; paper. **Milestones:** M1 (1–2) re-analysis + protocol; M2 (3–4) harness + 6 monitors; M3 (5–6) 12 monitors + report; 12 mo: adaptive tiers, external submissions, ControlArena integration. **Budget:** 6 mo 1 FTE + compute ≈ **$50–75k**; 12 mo 1.5 FTE ≈ **$150–225k**.

**Preliminary result ($0–20).** Re-analysis of the public DecisionRobust CSV with cluster CIs, selection sensitivity and robust-accuracy frontier (the +0.547 / +0.574 result is already reproduced, MEAS), plus 2–3 unmeasured models (Clef-flash open weights, MD1 API at ≈ $0.042/M tokens [EXT via W3], needs the directive exemption).

**Objections.** (1) *"Just a benchmark."* — Evaluation science is an explicit ask (AISI), and the protocol already yields findings (capability confound, missing models). (2) *"DecisionRobust exists."* — It is model-selected, non-adaptive for control, and lacks evidence-retention controls and cluster CIs; we complement and submit to it. (3) *"Why Jev-class models?"* — We evaluate generative monitors on identical units; the decision-model class is one comparator family.

### Framing B: Injection-robust gatekeepers with certified deferral for tool-calling agents

**Abstract.** Tool-calling agents are hijacked by instructions in tool outputs, and gatekeeper models that allow, deny or escalate each call are themselves steerable. We will build a gatekeeper whose decision is a bounded function of untrusted content over a trusted core (user task, policy, allow-list), certify per decision whether untrusted text can change the outcome, escalate otherwise, and apply cluster-valid finite-sample risk control to the auto-act set. We will evaluate under adaptive indirect injection with evidence-retention controls (benign tool guidance must still be followed), state all assumptions, and report where guarantees are vacuous.

**Threat model.** External attacker controls tool outputs (web, email, files); the gate sees user task, policy, proposed call, observation; attacker wants a harmful call allowed, or the gate flooded into escalation. *Severe-risk relevance:* agents with privileged tool access in cyber, financial and infrastructure settings; internal agents reading attacker-controlled data.

**Aims and deliverables.** Trusted-only information-gap study (go/no-go); G16-sym gate (permutation-symmetric) trained with injection and retention negatives; cluster-valid act/defer certificates; AgentDojo/InjecAgent-derived adaptive battery with an [ARGUS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03378)/[RAISED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06401)-style retention check; comparison with deterministic policy (CaMeL-style) and AttriGuard. **Milestones:** M1 (1–2) gap study + battery; M2 (3–4) gate v1; M3 (5–6) certificates + adaptive tiers; 12 mo: multi-step, second domain. **Budget:** 6 mo ≈ **$75–115k**; 12 mo ≈ **$200–300k**.

**Preliminary result ($0–25).** CPU trusted-only information gap on ≥ 2 agent suites (accuracy of a trusted-only classifier ≥ 85% of full ⇒ certificate useful); plus stock-4B gate steer-ASR with and without evidence-retention control (≤ $25).

**Objections.** (1) *"CaMeL/design patterns already give provable security."* — They need deterministic policies; this addresses learned gates where policies cannot decide, and measures what the certificate covers. (2) *"G16's veto is a host mask."* — Agreed (W3); certificates are claimed only for the trusted-resolvable region. (3) *"Certificates vacuous."* — That is the pre-registered falsifier; a vacuous result is reported.

### Framing D (exploratory): State-space monitors for long-horizon agent oversight

**Abstract.** Control monitors must read transcripts exceeding 500K tokens, and frontier LLM monitors miss dangerous actions 2–30× more often after 800K benign tokens. We will test whether recurrent/hybrid monitors that stream a transcript once with constant-size state and emit a decision at every action beat transformer monitors on detection per dollar at length, and measure the attack surface this creates: persistent-state poisoning and memory-collapse triggers authored by the monitored model. Deliverables: a long-transcript benchmark (needle and distributed attacks), a matched hybrid-vs-transformer comparison, a state-poisoning evaluation, and a snapshot-based erase-and-check procedure with measured GPU cost.

**Threat model.** (1) Needle after long benign context; (2) attacks fragmented across steps/agents ([Makins et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07368)); (3) U-authored text that overwrites or collapses the monitor's recurrent state (HiSPA/SpectralGuard). *Severe-risk relevance:* multi-step, multi-agent attacks needed for weight exfiltration or training-run poisoning.

**Why a funder might value it over A–C — and why not.** For: long-context monitors are an explicit AISI ask; incremental monitoring (monitor after every action) helped detection in context rot §4.5; streaming once at constant state is a natural fit (cheap forks, per-action reads); the HiSPA-in-hybrids question is open and monitor-specific. Against: SSMs have a recall problem — SSDM's own attention→GDN conversion collapsed recall (Index 35.8 → 14.1, memo §1) — and a monitor needs to *remember a rare bad action*, whereas decay bounds (‖A_t‖ < 1) forget it as well as injections [INFERENCE]. A grant reviewer will see D as a research bet; it should be a small add-on to A, not the pitch.

**Milestones.** M1 (1–3) transcript benchmark; M2 (4–6) hybrid vs transformer at 32K/128K/256K, per-action flag aggregation outside the state; 12 mo: HiSPA/GCG on GDN, snapshot erase-and-check on GPU. **Budget:** 6 mo ≈ **$55–85k**; 12 mo ≈ **$150–235k**.

**Preliminary result ($15–45).** (i) Needle-recall vs transcript length for Qwen3.5-4B (hybrid, 24 GDN + 8 attention layers per memo) against a same-size dense-transformer control [identity of control UNVERIFIED]; (ii) HiSPA triggers from the authors' RoBench-25 (code location stated in the abstract, [UNVERIFIED]) on both. **Kill:** hybrid recall at 128K below half the transformer's, or HiSPA-like triggers collapse the hybrid.

**Objections.** (1) *"SSMs forget; HiSPA exists."* — That is the tested hypothesis with a stated kill rule. (2) *"Long-context transformers make constant memory moot."* — Test cost per flagged action, not memory. (3) *"Stability bounds ≠ safety."* — Not claimed; flags aggregate outside the recurrence.

## 5. Verdict

**The current memo is not a good basis for safety grants as-is, but it is a good basis with changes.** Its headline identity (a Jev/Clef alternative chasing JevBench and Decision Index, with P1/P3/P4 as capability levers) will read as capability or commercial work to a control or security funder, and none of the six construction claims (a)–(f) is novel enough to carry a proposal alone. What is fundable is narrower and already in the memo: P2 plus the DAC — a pre-registered, paired, adaptive measurement of how steerable small typed monitors and gates are by the model they oversee, with evidence-retention controls, honest negatives and, secondarily, a hardened monitor — aimed at the monitor-robustness and long-trajectory-monitoring problems that AISI, Anthropic, Redwood and Open Phil explicitly name. The pitch must therefore lead with the control threat model (A, instrumented by C), treat B as a bounded follow-on and D as an exploratory add-on, and carry an explicit safety-tax and capability-confound analysis. The program is credible only if a preliminary result exists (the A manipulability gap) and if it admits its limits: the bounded-influence certificate is prior-only where the monitored text is the evidence, the invariance and act/defer machinery are table stakes, and the team has not yet shown a trained component that works.

**Drop from the pitch:** the Jev/Clef-alternative identity and "System One market" framing; JevBench Composite / Decision Index / Quyet targets; P1 as a capability recipe, P3, P4, Track L, P5-as-vendor; "far more aligned than Clef/MD1" and "more robust than Clef" (static templates only); "certified abstention", "injection-robust conformal sets" and the G16 veto as model alignment; state-space architecture as the *cause* of safety; νPI as a differentiator until a run exists; exact invariance as a headline (keep as a secondary property).

**Keep:** DAC and its evidence-retention axis; cluster-bootstrap, paired and leak-gate discipline; honest negative ledger; the open-attacker/open-teacher rules; the trusted-only information-gap falsifier (run first, CPU-only).
