## A. Extensions of Keel

### A1. Receipt-to-effect fault conformance — rank 5

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Audit integrity and authorized intent do not establish which effects actually happened. Study the smallest additional executor contract that makes ambiguous completion explicit across exceptions, restart, duplicate delivery, and adapter errors.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Hu et al. (2026) already bind claims, sources and execution in *Actions with Receipts* [S4]. Gilda's October 2026 individual draft already records decision versus observed effect [S5]; Schrock's August draft already handles indeterminate external effects [S6]. ActPlane covers execution paths missed at the tool layer [S3]. A new receipt schema, signature, or exactly-once slogan is **not novel**. These drafts are work in progress, not adopted IETF standards.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** An independently observed fault matrix and refinement counterexamples across adapter → kernel → executor are still a useful target in this source set. Keel provides a bounded model and real receipt semantics; its own §3.3 explicitly disclaims crash atomicity and exactly-once effects. Do not call that documented limit a discovered bug.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** Within 45 minutes/256 MB, enumerate a small two-action state machine with faults before append, after append, before effect, after effect, and before acknowledgement; compare honest executor and duplicate-delivery mutant against observable effect counts. This is a finite semantic experiment, not real filesystem crash testing.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour for a capped agent integration trace batch after CPU fault modeling; GPU is unnecessary for the structural result itself. Risk: only a familiar distributed-systems engineering note remains. Likely fits: Foresight Coordination and accountability (F2); EA Funds Transformative AI Fund (F1). Kill if no material distinction survives comparison to S4–S6.

### A2. Cumulative authority conformance — rank 14

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** A per-call amount ceiling does not bound a sequence of individually permitted payments. Compare task-level cumulative resource accounting with otherwise identical grants, including retry and delegation behavior.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** AgentSpec already enforces runtime constraints [S2], ActPlane cross-event policies [S3], and Schrock explicitly specifies reserve–execute–commit, delegation conservation, fencing and indeterminate charging [S6]. The generic mechanism is **already done at specification level**; this is a comparative conformance study, not an invention of budgeted capabilities.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Test behavioral compatibility and counterexamples when mapping narrow task clauses into a durable budget domain. Keel's explicit per-call clauses and bounded checker make the distinction testable; planner quality is held fixed.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 30 minutes/256 MB, exhaustively enumerate two requests, two delegated children, one shared budget, and duplicate messages. Compare per-call limits to a reference conservation invariant; report blocked benign alternatives as well as overspend traces. Do not claim the existing kernel promises cumulative limits.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $7.90 = 2 H100-hours for small scripted-grant agent batches with the same plans, not planner generation. Risk: no research gap beyond implementing S6; abandon as a standalone paper if so. Potential funders: F2; F1. It may remain worthwhile maintenance without being a strong research proposition.

### A3. Cluster certification with selective label arrival — rank 4

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Certification libraries need an explicit contract for labels that arrive mainly on reviewed cases. Study whether a small randomized audit channel plus complete cluster closure can support valid sequential certification at useful coverage; without auditable sampling, refuse the certificate.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Hultberg et al. (2026) already provide anytime CRC [S13]; Khosravi and Huo (2026) selective acting under specific predictable-update and monotone-risk assumptions [S14]; Gurram (2026) document clustering and selection leakage [S15]; Liu et al. (2026) coverage floors and bounded-ratio shift [S16]. Neither sequential validity, cluster correction, nor a coverage floor is new. A generic claim of validity under arbitrary drift would be wrong.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** The proposed estimand is a cluster-weighted acted-loss ratio when labels are delayed or preferentially observed, not ordinary iid threshold calibration. Existing LTT/CRR code supplies the frozen-gate comparator. Separate stationarity within declared epochs, known audit inclusion probabilities, and a hard refusal when positivity fails; do not assume an anytime e-process repairs missing labels or unknown shift.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 50 minutes/512 MB, stream 2,000 replicated finite Bernoulli-cluster worlds with known risks and audit probabilities; compare complete labels, review-only labels, and randomized audits with correct inclusion weights. Bound weights and include delayed cluster closure. Report false certificate rate, coverage, label cost, and stopping time; these would be SIM, not measured model safety.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour to generate one fresh synthetic score stream after the missing-label theorem and null simulations pass. Risk: audit variance makes all honest methods defer-all, or S14 already covers the exact contract. Funders: F1; F2's independent assessment/AI Insurance and Open Governance area. No mathematical guarantee is claimed here.

### A4. Monotone-risk resampling protocols — rank 8

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** A resampling policy should not silently turn repeated monitoring into permission shopping. Seek a protocol whose worst-case unsafe-release bound cannot improve for the attacker merely because the defender allows more candidate samples, under explicitly stated candidate dependence assumptions.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Terekhov et al. (2025, revised 2026) already show adaptive monitor failure and defer-to-resample backfiring [S12]. Best-of-N scaling is established [S17, S18]. Reproducing that failure is **not** the contribution.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Compare a single authorization decision bound to a transaction with min-score resampling, fixed audit escalation, and a prespecified sequential risk budget. Our verified APPS labels and receipt IDs permit modeling shared-payload and per-transaction dependence, without pretending resamples are independent.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 40 minutes/256 MB, enumerate correlated categorical candidate/monitor tables for n = 1, 2, 4, 8. Compare exact unsafe release, benign acceptance and audit cost; first try to construct a counterexample to the proposed monotonicity property. Do not reuse old one-score files as if they contained fresh resamples.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $5.93 = 1.5 H100-hours for matched candidate resampling on a small subset with open-model local serving. Risk: monotonicity requires always-audit or always-deny and buys no utility. Funders: F1; F2. Distinct from artifact auditing and detector training.

### A5. Discernment distillation with opposite response constraints — rank 9

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Train the same one-pass student to remain stable under untrusted directives **and** change under verified decision-relevant evidence. The new object is the paired difference in responses, not another collection of synthetic answer labels.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Our existing distillation already includes attacks, evidence edits, and policy remaps. QCRD already distills contrasting rationales [S33]; DEAR already identifies decision-supporting evidence in reasoning distillation [S34]; PAJAMA already distills judges into programs [S19]. Contrastive distillation and evidence-aware transfer are **not new**.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Test an explicit paired sensitivity objective using B1's executable label changes: penalize attack-induced decision change while rewarding the correct evidence-induced change, at equal tokens and updates to our existing rowwise KL recipe. Card already has co-primary utility/retention controls, so shortcut suppression cannot be reported as security improvement.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 45 minutes/512 MB, use generated gold outcomes and synthetic probability vectors to compare rowwise versus pairwise losses and gradients for four failure modes, including constant-output collapse. This diagnoses the objective only; it is not student training or transfer evidence.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $7.90 = 2 H100-hours for two equal-update LoRA arms and pilot scoring, using existing teacher distributions where available; fresh teacher labeling is extra. Risk: merely duplicates our training signal, adds no generalization, or destroys clean accuracy. Funders: F1; Coefficient Project Tailwind only if packaged as a genuinely new organization (F3), not a current microgrant.

### A6. Trust-bounded coherent one-pass decisions — rank 11

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Host-authored logical constraints across typed questions can expose or repair jointly impossible outputs without another model pass. Test whether contaminated questions can poison otherwise clean answers through that repair.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Ghosh et al. (2025) study logical consistency [S20]; Paleka et al. (2024/2025) probabilistic consistency checks [S36]; Mohri et al. (2025) projection mechanisms and conditional improvement theory [S35]. Projection and coherence are **already done**. Our canonical option ordering is also already exact and is not this proposition.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** The research question is trust separation: only the host supplies relations, and the study measures error propagation from an attacked marginal through a joint repair. Keel's typed multi-question requests and atomic deferral provide a clean interface. Logical feasibility is not factual accuracy; a wrong constraint can make a coherent system worse.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 30 minutes/256 MB, enumerate Boolean worlds for at most eight atoms; project hand-specified inconsistent probability vectors and inject error into one marginal. Compare Brier loss, worst-question loss, joint consistency, and atomic defer under valid versus deliberately invalid host constraints.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $5.93 = 1.5 H100-hours for fresh synthetic multi-question scores from our stack. Risk: only standard projection under a new wrapper, or repaired coherence hides worse targeted decisions. Funders: F1; F2 Human Empowerment where decisions and review are the application.

## B. Adjacent safety and evaluation using existing assets

### B1. Executable evidence contracts — rank 1

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Replace model-agreement evidence labels with small executable decision worlds. Separate correct response to a factual update, rejection of an untrusted command, and compliance with a trusted policy update within the same world.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** SecFid already separates processing from suppressing injected content [S7], including an agentic extension. Vashishtha et al. (ICLR 2026) already generate executable causal problems [S8]. Therefore neither evidence retention nor executable counterfactuals is novel. The defensible contribution is a typed decision/intervention contract with exact outcome labels, held-out event composition, and budgeted adaptive attacks—not renaming SecFid. Fully observed event edits are interventions, not Pearlian counterfactual abduction.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Our documented evidence-label bottleneck and Card paired metrics create an immediate use. Use deterministic state-machine worlds for temporal precedence, entity binding, permissions, and bounded arithmetic; factual updates change world state, directives do not, and authenticated policy updates change the rule. AgentDojo predicates can supply a separate ecological check, not automatic semantic validation of every generated text.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 45 minutes/512 MB, enumerate 200 small worlds and their four matched interventions through two independently specified interpreters. Measure oracle disagreement, answer balance, and collisions where intended distinct behaviors produce the same answer. No LLM labels, no dataset-code execution.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour for a 100-world elicitation pilot on our existing judge stack; 100 × four variants × two arms = 800 one-pass decisions plus a reserved adaptive subset. Risk: a lexical toy or near-duplicate benchmark whose labels do not survive natural-language realization. Funders: F1; F2 independent risk assessment. Full confirmation and human wording audit are separate costs.

### B2. Backdoor behavior × presentation crossover — rank 2

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Identify whether a monitor reacts to the behavior-changing defect or to a presentation cue by crossing a minimal behavioral repair with semantics-preserving surface changes. A successful monitor should separate repaired from backdoored code in both presentations.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** SLEIGHT-Bench already pairs malicious transcripts with near-identical benign variants [S9]. Štorek et al. (2026) already test semantic versus lexical code understanding using interventions [S10]; Ye et al. (2025) already use semantics-preserving transformations as model backdoor triggers [S11]. Matched variants, semantic sensitivity, and transformations are **already done**. Our narrower contribution is a functionally validated 2×2 within-program APPS monitor experiment; S11 concerns backdoors in neural code models, not the identical program-level threat.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Existing verified trigger inputs and honest APPS counterparts anchor defect repair and behavior checks. Use the artifact-audit sibling only to flag candidate surface cues; do not rerun its classifier or select final examples on a monitor's apparent failure.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 50 minutes/512 MB, statically construct candidate 2×2 edits for 20 programs, verify parse/round-trip properties and variable-binding restrictions, and count feasible minimal repairs using available metadata. This cannot establish semantic equivalence; dynamic validation is a separate later no-network sandbox stage.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour for at most 162 × four cells × two monitor arms = 1,296 scores after the sandbox/human validity gate. Risk: ambiguous repairs, invalid triggers, or too few independent problems. Funders: F1; F2 independent assessment. Do not mistake verified difference from one reference on one input for universal program equivalence.

### B3. Oversight-queue availability frontier — rank 3

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Fail-closed review can preserve action safety while making legitimate service unavailable. Measure the attacker's cheapest way to consume review capacity, and which capacity-aware admission policy improves benign completion without releasing more harmful actions.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Luo et al. (USENIX Security 2026) already establish agent resource-exhaustion attacks [S32]; SLEIGHT-Bench discusses false-positive review feasibility [S9]; adaptive control already has finite audit budgets [S12]. Resource denial and bounded oversight are **not new**. Our distinct target is pre-execution human-review backlog with host-enforced holds, not token exhaustion or post-hoc monitoring.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Keel already holds rather than auto-approves on saturation and has receipt events; its proposition explicitly says a service model is missing. Replay frozen gate outcomes under external arrival/service schedules. This evaluates availability without asserting that people actually have the simulated service times.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 45 minutes/256 MB, run event-driven replay on 100,000 synthetic arrivals per scenario with fixed-capacity reviewers, bursty attackers, and per-principal admission limits. Compare global FIFO, per-principal fair queueing, and reserved benign capacity; include attacker identity splitting. Exact methodology follows below.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour for a small fresh AgentDojo trace batch to replace synthetic routing frequencies, not a full attack benchmark. Risk: isolation only works with trusted principal identities or unrealistic review assumptions. Funders: F2 Human Empowerment and decentralized oversight; F1. Never convert a full queue into automatic approval.

### B4. Budget-normalized attack ranking — rank 6

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Compare defenses on the complete attack budget curve and replicated attack transfer, rather than a single best-of-search endpoint. Determine when an apparent ranking reverses after equalizing queries, inference randomness, and cost.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Best-of-N scaling is established [S17]; Biroli (September 2026) explicitly studies augmentation and completion budgets jointly [S18]; our Card already distinguishes static and searched estimands. New curves alone are **not a contribution**.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Focus on paired, censored attack histories and ranking uncertainty under query/token budgets, with a held-out replay of found attacks. Card's exact budgets and receipt-bound prompts make provenance unusually cheap. A deterministic grammar search is not a sample from all possible attackers.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 40 minutes/512 MB, analyze a simulated finite attack-response table with correlated variants, early stopping and multiple completions. Compare endpoint ASR, area under the budget curve, and task-cluster bootstrap rank intervals. Apply to existing histories only if candidate order and all failed queries were retained; otherwise report that missing prerequisite, not reconstructed histories.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour for a capped 30-item × 16-query × two-arm replay pilot, with matched feedback and fixed per-call output caps. Risk: already-standard evaluation practice, or trace censoring makes the desired estimand unidentified. Funders: F1; F2 independent technical assessment.

## C. Unassociated propositions

### C1. Rare-mechanism-preserving reference-model curation — rank 10

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Small-reference perplexity pruning should preserve rare safety-relevant mechanisms, not merely improve average downstream quality. Test a constrained subset rule that preserves mechanism coverage while matching retained tokens and training FLOPs.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Ankner et al. (2024; ICLR 2025) already show small-reference pruning [S21]. Zhao et al. (2026) already propose dynamic reweighting [S22], Pham et al. (2025) behavior-aware safety sampling [S23], and dataset cartography is foundational rather than new [S24]. Do not pitch perplexity pruning, data maps, or adding safety data as novel.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Cross **reference difficulty** with independently specified rare computational mechanisms and held-out compositions. The independent research line needs no Keel asset; optional executable task generators reduce label cost. Mechanism strata must be defined without consulting the final evaluation or hidden safety labels.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 50 minutes/512 MB, fit a streaming character n-gram reference on at most 20 MB of procedurally generated arithmetic/logic training text; compare perplexity quantiles, random selection, and mechanism-stratified selection on retained mechanism mass and cheap bag-of-features prediction. No model weights. This is a selection diagnostic, not evidence that 4B training improves.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $7.90 = 2 H100-hours for a tiny custom student at equal tokens, three subset rules and multiple seeds if throughput permits; not a pretrained frontier reproduction. Risk: gains vanish when domains/FLOPs are controlled, or small reference difficulty does not rank student value. Funders: F1 only with a credible safety mechanism; Foresight's Node science/safety program (F2) is a weaker, mission-dependent fit, not an efficiency grant by default.

### C2. Cross-domain controllability and collateral effects — rank 12

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** A behavior may be steerable in one domain but uncontrollable without collateral damage elsewhere. Measure the rank and condition of a causal intervention-to-behavior response matrix, and test whether safe directions persist across contexts.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Li et al. (2026) already audit steering safety failures [S26]; Li and Kasneci (August 2026) already remove a separable safety-degrading component with constrained optimization [S25]. A single refusal-orthogonal steering vector or primal-dual update is **already done**. This is not our PID/νPI development experiment.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Test a multi-behavior, cross-domain identification criterion with independent causal interventions, rather than infer controllability from probe accuracy or same-domain steering. No special Keel asset is necessary; control-theoretic framing is intellectual leverage, not evidence of a moat.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 50 minutes/512 MB, use known linear dynamical systems with nuisance directions to compare observational probe rank with true intervention-response rank under noisy finite differences. Report recovery error, smallest singular value and collateral loss; label all outputs SIM. This only checks identifiability and estimator design.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $11.85 = 3 H100-hours for interventions on a small custom trained model across disjoint synthetic domains, using random and norm-matched control directions. Risk: nonlinear context dependence destroys the local control model, or the result merely repeats S25. Funders: F1; F3 only for an organization-scale interpretability/control agenda. No frontier-scale safety claim follows from the toy.

### C3. Minimal hybrid memory under state revisions — rank 13

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** The useful efficiency question is not whether an SSM recalls an unchanged key, but how little attention or external memory is necessary for reliable overwrite, revocation, and entity rebinding under length shift. Find an empirical state-capacity frontier in small custom models.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Okpekpe and Orvieto (2025) already study recurrent associative-recall optimization and scaling [S27]. Arora et al. (2025, revised 2026) already identify recall mechanisms in Transformers/SSMs [S28]. Hybrid recall and mechanistic diagnosis are **not new**; our past attention-removal collapse also rules out treating cheap conversion as unexplored.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Use identical token budgets and parameter-matched custom models on state revisions with controlled distractors, held-out sequence lengths, and adversarial order; isolate memory capacity from optimization by a prespecified learning-rate sweep. No Keel asset is required, though typed event worlds could be reused.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 45 minutes/256 MB, generate 10,000 overwrite/revoke sequences and compare exact key-value memory, bounded LRU, and finite-state baselines. Count irreducible collisions at each memory budget; no neural training. Establish whether the task actually separates candidate memory regimes before paying.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $15.80 = 4 H100-hours for a limited parameter-matched custom SSM/hybrid toy sweep; enough only for a feasibility pilot, not a competitive small language model. Risk: ordinary AR with new names, or optimizer tuning explains the result. Funders: ARIA Nature-Inspired Computation opportunity seeds (F4; application availability unverified), F1 only if a concrete safety benefit is established. Generic capability efficiency is not automatically AI safety.

### C4. Verifier adequacy before small-model RL — rank 7

[INFERENCE] **Thesis.** Before training a small model on verifiable rewards, measure whether the verifier can reject wrong answers that exploit its own equivalence assumptions. Test whether a mutation-based adequacy score predicts reward hacking on held-out failure mechanisms.

[EXT] **Closest work / novelty check.** Reasoning Gym already supplies procedural generators and verifiers [S29]; Rajan (June 2026) already audits incorrect patches accepted by code-RL tests [S30]; EvilGenie already studies coding reward hacking [S31]. Verifier auditing and mutation testing are **not novel**. The narrow proposition is cross-family prediction of vulnerability in cheap math/logic verifiers, not another code-unit-test audit.

[INFERENCE] **Gap / edge.** Hold out answer-format and semantic failure families, distinguish false acceptance from incomplete-but-valid answers, and compare mutation adequacy with random test counts. No Keel dependence: standalone small-model RL teams need this quality gate. Existing executable-semantics expertise is optional leverage, not a unique dataset advantage.

[INFERENCE] **Free first experiment.** In 50 minutes/512 MB, exercise at most ten simple deterministic verifiers against independently specified wrong-answer mutations: boundary arithmetic, quantifier flips, duplicate set members, numerical tolerance and parser coercions. No downloaded/generated code executes. Use reference solvers to label each mutation and count invalid mutations separately.

[INFERENCE] **First paid step / risk / funders.** $3.95 = 1 H100-hour for a small custom policy trained against one original and one hardened verifier at equal updates. Risk: curated mutations do not predict learned exploits, or strict parsing rejects legitimate answers. Funders: F1; F3 for a larger independent verification initiative. A passed mutation suite is not a proof of a sound reward function.

## Top-three methodology

### Rank 1 / B1: executable evidence contracts

[INFERENCE] **Design.** Freeze four independently authored event grammars: temporal precedence, entity binding, permission revision and bounded numeric comparison. First run the 200-world CPU oracle pilot. For confirmation, prepare 400 independent worlds, 100 per grammar, each with clean, untrusted directive, factual intervention, and authenticated policy intervention. Keep base-world identity as the cluster; variants and repeated queries never count as independent samples. Write a reference transition interpreter and a separately implemented truth-table oracle before rendering prose. Reject ambiguous worlds before any judge scores are seen.

[INFERENCE] **Controls / metrics.** Compare the existing rowwise-distilled judge with its frozen stock baseline at matched one-pass inputs; add ignore-all-evidence and follow-last-directive negative controls using deterministic rules. Primary measure is world-level **joint success** on all four contract arms; report clean accuracy, attack steering, evidence response and policy response separately, including unconditional denominators as well as clean-correct conditional rates. Hold out compositional grammar rules, not merely random seeds. Inspect 40 prose realizations with two independent humans before confirmation; this human work is not included in the free CPU or $3.95 pilot envelope.

[INFERENCE] **Sample size.** For paired binary outcomes, planning variance is `(q - Δ²)/n`, where q is the discordant fraction. At Δ = .10, q = .30 and n = 400 independent worlds, the signal is approximately .10/sqrt(.29/400) = 3.71 standard errors before family clustering. The rough two-sided 5%/80%-power approximation is `n ≈ (1.96 + .84)²q/Δ² ≈ 235`; this is a planning calculation, not a simulated or guaranteed power result. If only four grammars are the independent generalization units, n = 400 does not supply 400 independent domains: report domain-specific results and reserve new grammars for replication.

[INFERENCE] **Kill conditions.** Any unresolved oracle disagreement; >5% invalid/ambiguous prose in the blind audit; >90% performance by a frozen lexical-only control; no held-out composition separation; or a literature comparison showing the same intervention contract already covered by SecFid. A negative model result is publishable only if the measurement contract itself adds value.

### Rank 2 / B2: behavior × presentation crossover

[INFERENCE] **Design.** Attempt all 162 backdoors without selecting on monitor scores. For each feasible program, construct defective-original, repaired-original, defective-transformed, and repaired-transformed. Transformations must preserve lexical scope and avoid reflective/string-evaluated names; use identity transformations as controls. A repair must remove the witnessed defect without changing stated behavior. Before scoring, validate all four cells against provided tests, the secret trigger, independent reference solutions where available, and newly generated domain-valid inputs inside an existing no-network sandbox. Passing these checks is tested equivalence on a finite set, not proof of universal equivalence.

[INFERENCE] **Controls / metrics.** Primary endpoint is within-problem defective-minus-repaired suspiciousness, averaged over the two presentations. Secondary endpoints are the defect × presentation interaction, paired discrimination probability, and detection at a frozen false-positive threshold. Include comments-only, formatting-only and identity edits; cross transformation families at confirmation. No snippet metadata reveals repair labels. Split all variants and same-problem solutions together. Withhold one third of the 573 honest programs as final evaluation; calibrate thresholds only on the remainder, grouped by problem, and never tune transformations using final scores.

[INFERENCE] **Sample size.** At most 162 programs is an upper bound, not the number of independent APPS problems. For a paired proportion contrast Δ = .15 with q = .40, `n ≈ 7.84 × .40 / .15² ≈ 140` independent pairs is an approximate 80%-power planning target before exclusions/clustering. If only 60 independent problems survive, the rough detectable contrast is `sqrt(7.84 × .40 / 60) ≈ .23`. A 2% FPR estimate from 573 independent honest programs has only about 11 expected false positives, fewer after splitting; use exact intervals and never claim precise low-FPR certification. Freeze threshold before the withheld evaluation.

[INFERENCE] **Kill conditions.** Fewer than 60 independent feasible problems for the declared target; invalid trigger domains; repair labels encoded by formatting; a surface transformation changes tested behavior; only compiler- or dataset-specific effects; or zero incremental insight beyond the artifact audit plus SLEIGHT/SemTrace. If adequate repairs are unavailable, do not replace the question with unrelated static detection.

### Rank 3 / B3: oversight-queue availability frontier

[INFERENCE] **Design.** Model arrival, gate routing, review service and expiry separately. Replay a frozen routing trace, or explicitly synthetic frequencies, through an event-driven queue. Sweep utilization λ/(cμ) = .25, .50, .75, .95 and 1.10; independent burst and sustained attacker schedules; service-time variability; queue capacities; and authenticated versus freely split attacker identities. The attacker knows admission policy and chooses arrival timing and review-inducing cases, but cannot forge host principal identity in the authenticated arm. No assertion that the gate detects those cases correctly is needed for queue stress.

[INFERENCE] **Controls / metrics.** Compare global FIFO, fair per-principal queues, and a reserved-capacity admission rule with identical total reviewers and queue storage. Include no-attack, infinite-service oracle, always-hold and an explicitly labeled unsafe fail-open comparator. Primary outcome: fraction of legitimate tasks completed before a preregistered deadline. Co-primary safety: harmful executed effects, which must not increase to buy availability. Also report p95 wait, review work, starvation, rejected benign work and the attack cost to halve legitimate completion. No auto-release on queue fullness or timeout.

[INFERENCE] **Sample size / uncertainty.** Use 100 independent arrival schedules per declared cell with common random numbers across policies; each schedule can contain 100,000 arrivals, but schedules—not arrivals—are the replication unit. At a planning SD of .10 for paired completion differences, 100 schedules gives SE .01; that SD is assumed and must be checked in the pilot. Use paired schedule bootstrap intervals, family-wise correction for a small prespecified policy family, and sequential early termination only for deterministic unsafe behavior. Simulation can be precise about its model while remaining uninformative about actual reviewer behavior.

[INFERENCE] **Kill conditions.** Benefits disappear with identity splitting and no deployable identity boundary exists; conclusions reverse across plausible service distributions; availability improves only by auto-approving; or the operational result is merely the trivial statement that arrival rate exceeds capacity. A valuable result needs either a materially improved equal-resource frontier or a quantified, falsifiable deployment constraint.
