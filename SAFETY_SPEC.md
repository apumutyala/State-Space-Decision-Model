# SAFETY_SPEC — defined safety and alignment guarantees (v1, 2026-10-06)

This is the missing core document. The repository already holds research (`techniques/01–08`), measured prior-lab results (`reviews/02`, `reviews/03`), a contract (`interfaces/DECISION_LANGUAGE_V0.md`) and a pre-registration (`safety_eval/PREREG.md`, PR #8). What it did not hold is a single list of the guarantees we are actually making, each stated exactly as proven, with its tier, assumptions, test, component, and what Clef and Jev offer on the same row. This file is that list. **Every row is a target until it is individually discharged by the receipt its row names; nothing in this file is claimed as achieved.** The file is subordinate to `GOAL.md`: where a row proposes a statistical margin (G13), that margin is a proposed test of the goal's "no regression" wording and needs the user's approval; it does not amend the goal. A guarantee is claimed in a results PR only in the words of its row, and a row's tier is reached only by its receipt. A STRUCTURAL row needs a code-path argument plus tests; finite tests alone do not establish universal correctness.

Companion: `techniques/09-safety-research-2026.md` holds the 2025–26 literature behind the novelty column, with a verification status for every citation. Citations below are arXiv IDs; all were fetched this session unless marked **[unverified]** there.

Tiers used throughout:

| Tier | Meaning | How it is discharged |
|---|---|---|
| **STRUCTURAL** | Holds by construction of the host-side code path, for every input, independent of model weights | A test suite that exercises the boundary plus a fixture that tries to break it; the guarantee is as good as the code and its stated assumptions, nothing more |
| **CERTIFIED** | Proved for a stated bounded perturbation class on a per-decision basis, with ABSTAIN outside the certified set | A certificate computation that is itself tested against a brute-force oracle on small cases |
| **STATISTICAL** | A bound on a risk that holds with stated probability under stated distributional assumptions | Calibration-set receipt, the bound's parameters (α, δ, n), and a stress test that shows where the assumption breaks |
| **EMPIRICAL** | A measurement with a confidence interval under a pre-registered adaptive-attack protocol; no bound is claimed | Receipt per `COORDINATION.md`: command, hashes, seeds, cluster-bootstrap CI, raw predictions; independent recomputation |

## 1. Thesis

Our safety edge is not that the model is safer. It is that every safety-relevant property is placed where it can either be enforced by deterministic host code or measured under adaptive attack, and never where it would have to be trusted to the weights. The 2026 theory says this is the only place such properties can live: within a shared-embedding sequence model that lacks an immutable provenance-enforcing mechanism, perfect prompt-injection prevention is impossible (2606.27567), and even with instruction/data separation an adversary can always construct a context that makes a bad flow look legitimate (2605.17634). So the learned model (Track D) only chooses among options the host supplied, with probabilities the host can calibrate, abstain on and certify; the host decides what the options are, which text is data, which audience may see what, and what a text answer is allowed to mean (Track L). Clef and Jev stop at the first of these: they guarantee that the output is a valid choice over the schema. We guarantee that plus: authority cannot be acquired from text (fail-closed), public outputs cannot depend on private evidence (by construction of the input), a decision can be certified unchanged under bounded untrusted insertions or else escalated, acted-on decisions carry a bounded selective risk with the correct denominator, every text answer asserts only what a deterministic kernel checked against the evidence, and the open residual — the wrong-but-allowed option under a one-sentence, white-box or black-box adaptive attack, confidence under attack, and (new for recurrent models) hidden-state poisoning — is measured with CIs on a held-out split against pre-registered gates, with the same harness run on Clef and Clef-flash. Where a property cannot be made structural, certified or bounded, Section 5 says so instead of implying it.

## 2. Threat model

**Who attacks.** (i) Authors of untrusted content that ends up inside the request's `state`: records, documents, tool outputs, prior turns. (ii) Semi-trusted callers who supply question instructions and option descriptions (schema text is data for authority purposes; it is never an instruction channel). (iii) Training-data suppliers: scraped corpora, teacher outputs (open-weight teachers only), synthetic generators. (iv) Operators who misconfigure policy, audience or provenance: out of scope for the model guarantees, in scope for fail-closed validation. (v) A scheming model: out of scope. Nothing here assumes the weights are adversarial and nothing here is an AI-control protocol (2312.06942, 2511.02997); a typed decision model has no chain of thought to monitor or to obfuscate (2605.15257, 2608.04735), which removes one oversight channel and one evasion channel at once, and we claim neither as a safety property.

**Channels.** `state` (untrusted by default); option descriptions and question instructions (data for authority; attack surface for steering); tool outputs (a kind of `state`); training data (poisoning); reused state handles (contamination across authority or audience); diagnostics, error and repair text (leakage, including denial-feedback leakage, 2604.04035); the realizer's output medium (markup injection); **the recurrent state itself** (short trigger phrases that make a Mamba-class model forget its context: hidden-state poisoning, 2601.01972, 2603.12414, 2604.16424); timing (excluded from every claim).

**Harms**, each mapped to the rows that address it:

| Harm | Definition | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Forbidden action (hijack) | The executed option is outside the host's allowed set | G1, G2, G6 |
| Wrong allowed action (steer) | The executed option is allowed but is the attacker's target, not the correct one | G4, G5, G6 |
| Confident wrong | The served probability on a wrong executed option is high, or confidence is moved without the decision moving (2401.02718) | G5, G7 |
| Authority escalation | Text in any field becomes an instruction, a control token, a policy restriction or a proof of acceptance | G2, G11 |
| Private leakage | A public-audience output, diagnostic or repair message depends on, or reveals the existence of, private evidence | G3, G11 |
| Unsupported assertion | A rendered text asserts a proposition the evidence and registered rules do not support, or whose meaning differs from the checked program | G10, G11 |
| Silent degradation | Truncation, option pruning, model fallback or partial answers without an explicit status | G8 |
| Memory collapse | Untrusted content makes the recurrent state lose the trusted context (schema, question, earlier records) so later decisions degrade | G14 |
| Regression during growth | A later checkpoint or language level loses decision competence or safety relative to the accepted one | G12, G13 |

What is not a harm here: refusals and abstentions are costs (coverage), reported beside every guarantee that produces them; the Decision Index scores an abstention as wrong, so G4 and G5 are deployment features and are never on a submitted Index run. Membership inference on typed outputs (shown against Jev, 2610.04985) is a privacy threat we do not address in v1 beyond G3's scope statement.

## 5. What we will NOT claim, and why

1. **"Provably safe", "aligned", "no hallucination".** G10 is evidence-relative soundness; it does not establish truth of premises, relevance, completeness or harmless consequences. The prior lab's claim ledger already rejects "constrained decoding or certificates guarantee semantic correctness" (`reviews/03` §3). Jev's "0 % hallucination" is a schema statement; we will not make the same rhetorical move.
2. **Architecture-level safety from the SSM.** No half-life, Gramian, contraction or spectral-radius bound is a safety claim: per-step spectral bounds fail for switching systems (product radius 1.20 from two radius-0.99 transitions), half-life bounds are vacuous at useful memory lengths, and the operator-norm cap that does hold measurably worsened adherence (`reviews/02` §4–6, `reviews/03` §1.4). The 2026 result that a contraction-constrained safety head admits IBP certificates (2610.02853) is real and small-model; we treat it as an arm with a tax (G14), not a property of the parent. "Attention-free" is a cost and latency property, not a safety property; recurrent state is an *extra* attack surface (G14), not a smaller one.
3. **Learned defences as guarantees.** Injection-augmented LoRA, ISE/ASIDE channels, datamarking and on-policy distillation (SecOPD, 2608.21500) are empirical (G6). Every in-band defence that has been attacked adaptively has broken (85–95 % ASR on StruQ/SecAlign; 12 defences at > 90 %), 2606.27567 says the shared-embedding path cannot be made perfect, and 2605.17634 says contextual manipulation survives separation; we expect our learned arms to reduce, not remove, steering, and the pre-registered fold rule says what happens if they do not.
4. **Certified ⇒ correct.** A G4 certificate says the decision did not depend on ≤ k inserted sentences. The prior lab measured 57.6 % certified-but-wrong on Qwen3-4B and gave a two-world proof that a decision resting on one indispensable sentence cannot be certified by erasure. We publish the certified-but-wrong rate next to every certificate rate.
5. **Exact certificates against edits.** G4's exact certificate covers insertions only. Substitutions and deletions of true evidence are covered only by the probabilistic CERT-ED arm when it exists, and by G6 otherwise.
6. **Selective-risk bounds under attack-goal shift.** G5 holds within a stratum under exchangeability. Realised risk on held-out goals is reported as a stress test; it is outside the bound and we will not call it "controlled". If the feasibility floor says a stratum cannot be certified at α without abstaining on most inputs, we say that too.
7. **Noninterference beyond the text distribution.** G3 excludes timing, operational side channels, and private data already in the weights (membership inference against typed outputs is demonstrated on Jev, 2610.04985). "The final program mentions only public facts" is not noninterference; the choice among public facts can leak, which is why v0 forbids private-dependent planning entirely.
8. **Plain-text role spoofing.** No encoder stops a `state` that contains "SYSTEM:" or "END STATE". G2 stops control tokens; spoofing is measured under G6.
9. **Attribution as explanation.** G9(a) is arithmetic. Causal localisation in SSMs is non-unique (2606.00930), faithfulness metrics are unreliable in general (2605.25052), and "100 % of decision-relevant information passes through h_sem" is vacuous and will not be said (`reviews/02` §2).
10. **Relative safety vs Clef or Jev without running them.** Any "better than" sentence about Clef or Clef-flash requires the Section 7 baseline on our harness, independently recomputed. Any sentence about Jev is limited to published numbers unless the user approves API evaluation under TypeSafe's terms (Section 7).
11. **Vision or multimodal paths.** All rows are text-only.
12. **Numbers from smoke runs or stock ≤ 1B models** are pipeline qualification, never model evidence (`PREREG` §0).
13. **Control against a scheming model, or oversight via chain of thought.** Nothing here is an AI-control protocol; the threat model assumes honest weights and adversarial inputs. A typed decision model emits no reasoning text, so CoT monitoring neither helps nor can be evaded; we claim no oversight benefit from that.

## 7. Baselines: Clef and Clef-flash on our harness; Jev

No "better than Clef" sentence is allowed without the following, and it is budgeted as a joint-gate item:

1. **Adapter parity first (m0), as two separate gates.** (a) *Wire-answer arithmetic:* PR #6's recomputation with Cloudflare's `systemone_answer` logic on recorded logits establishes that our answer wiring matches theirs for the stock readout — and nothing more. (b) *Head parity, still future:* run Cloudflare's unchanged `joint_schema_model.py` head (Apache-2.0; weights on the Hub) inside a `decide()` backend and match its served distributions on the replay fixture to the reference implementation's outputs within a declared tolerance. (a) does not establish (b); no G-row is measured on Clef or Clef-flash until (b) has its own receipt.
2. **Rows measured on Clef-flash (9B) and Clef (27B):** G1 (does the head ever emit an invalid distribution?), G2(b) (send untrusted text containing `<|im_start|>` and other Qwen added tokens; measure hijack before and after our encoder — the prior lab's 78.7 % → 2.0 % result on Qwen3.6-27B is the prior), G6 (full PREREG battery: D1-B, hijack best-of-5, GCG-lite, black-box search; both-orders criterion), G7 (debiased ECE clean and attacked-not-steered on the ≥ 10k dev slice and the safety split; calibration-attack arm), G8 (over-length and > 255-option requests: does it truncate or prune silently?), G4 (erase-and-check cost at k = 1 on the D1-port, so the certificate's escalation rate is compared like for like), **G14** (Clef-flash is a GDN/attention hybrid on the same backbone family; the HiSPA result on Jamba-1.7-Mini makes this the single most informative baseline row). G3, G10, G11 do not apply (no audience, no language), and that absence is the comparison.
3. **Protocol.** Same test split, same seeds, same cluster-bootstrap draws; paired differences (candidate − Clef) with CIs; recomputed by the other agent from raw predictions before any PR says "better".
4. **Cost.** Clef-flash fits one H100 in bf16 (≈ 18 GB); the battery is ≈ 3–4 H100-h (≈ $9–12). Clef-27B ≈ 54 GB, ≈ 2×. Both inside the $75 joint gate's remaining balance after m0; a `spend` issue is opened before either runs. Cloudflare publishes no safety numbers of its own (changelog, blog, model cards and Workers AI docs were all checked on 2026-10-06); the widely quoted "5.9 % grant / 76 % social engineering" figure is a third-party blog test and is not cited as a Cloudflare claim.
5. **Jev — what the terms say and what we do.** TypeSafe's Master Customer Agreement (typesafe.ai/legal/mca, last updated 2026-09-23) and Terms of Use (2026-09-19) contain **no clause mentioning benchmarking, evaluation, comparative results or publication**. §4.2 assigns ownership of Output to the Customer. Reading: scoring Jev on a public benchmark and publishing the comparison is not expressly prohibited (Cloudflare published exactly such comparisons), but using Jev outputs to build, tune, or "facilitate" a competing decision model is barred, and the Acceptable Use Policy is unread. **Rule:** Jev is never a teacher, label source or reference distribution.
